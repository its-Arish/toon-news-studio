Look at the sign at your gas station. A year ago it said three dollars and seven cents. This spring it went past four and a half. And this month, for the first time since July, it dropped back under four. Oil has fallen three weeks in a row. So the war must be ending, right?

Not even close. Because the price of oil right now is not really about oil. It is about a war that Congress never voted for. An election that just flipped Washington upside down. And three million pages of files with one name in them that will not go away. By the end of this, you will know why oil is falling, why the next six weeks may be the most dangerous of this whole war, and what the number on that gas sign has to do with Jeffrey Epstein.

Let's go back to the start of this year. On the 30th of January, the Justice Department released more than three million pages of the Epstein files. The President's name appears in them many times. Being named in those files is not proof of a crime, and the Justice Department said some of the claims about him were untrue and sensational. But the files were the biggest story in America. A month later, on the 28th of February, the United States and Israel launched strikes on Iran. They began just hours after Bill Clinton had testified to Congress about Epstein. The war was launched without a vote in Congress.

Within days, a strange idea spread across both parties. Republican congressman Thomas Massie, one of the men who forced the files out, said that bombing a country on the other side of the world would not make the Epstein files go away. Democrats like Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez said the same. People online started calling the war Operation Epstein Fury. One poll in March, from a left-leaning group, found more than half of voters thought the war was at least partly a distraction. The White House called that idea ridiculous, and said only true morons could believe it.

Whatever the reason, the war was real, and so was the bill. Before that morning, a barrel of oil cost about seventy dollars. Iran answered with the one weapon that scares every economy on earth. It shut the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow strip of water between Iran and Oman that carries close to a fifth of the world's oil. Tanker traffic fell to almost nothing. The International Energy Agency called it the largest supply disruption in history. By the end of April, oil hit one hundred and twenty-six dollars, the highest in four years. American gas jumped a full dollar in a single month.

And here is where the politics and the price became the same story. A ceasefire came, then talks, then a deal on paper in June. It fell apart within weeks, over who controls the strait. In July, the US Navy put its blockade of Iranian ports back in place, and Iran kept hitting ships. October became the deadliest month for attacks on shipping in the whole war. Meanwhile, back home, the Epstein files never left. The Attorney General, Pam Bondi, was pushed out in April after criticism over how she handled them. The Commerce Secretary, Howard Lutnick, became the first sitting cabinet member to be questioned by Congress about his ties to Epstein. And every week, Americans paid more at the pump. One poll found that sixty-three percent of Americans blamed the President for high gas prices.

Then came the 3rd of November.

The midterm elections were not close. Democrats took the House. Democrats took the Senate, flipping nine seats, the most for any party in nearly seventy years. They will hold fifty-six seats in the Senate and well over two hundred and fifty in the House. It has been called the biggest blue wave since Watergate. And the war was a big part of why. A Reuters and Ipsos poll this month found seventy-one percent of Americans want Congress to pass a law ending it, including about half of Republicans.

Watch what happened next. Within a week, the President said the war was militarily effectively over and that new strikes were not needed. Oil dropped below ninety dollars for the first time since August. Gas fell under four. So is that why oil is falling? Partly. But the bigger reason is something most people missed.

The oil found another way out. Tankers started slipping through the southern side of the strait with their trackers switched off, many of them guided by American forces. Gulf countries pushed more crude through pipelines that skip Hormuz completely. By October, Gulf crude exports were back above eighty percent of their pre-war level. And because the war made oil so expensive, the world burned less of it. On the 13th of November, the International Energy Agency said supply is now bigger than demand for the first time since the war began. That is why the price is falling. Not because the war ended. Because the world learned to live around it.

And the war has not ended. Ship traffic through Hormuz is still about sixty-five percent below normal. Tankers are still being hit. A third American aircraft carrier just arrived in the region. CBS News reports Israel and the United States are considering a new assault on Iran. Iran has set seven conditions before it reopens the strait, starting with an end to the American blockade. And just days ago, the Senate rejected another attempt to limit the President's war powers, forty-eight to fifty-one.

At the very same moment, the Epstein files came roaring back. On the 11th of November, Thomas Massie got the two hundred and eighteen signatures he needed to force a new House vote to release more Epstein files, because survivors and lawmakers say records are still being held back. On Thursday, Marjorie Taylor Greene, once one of the President's loudest allies, claimed that he personally blocked the files. That is her claim, and it has not been proven. But it tells you how much his own side has split.

So now you have a President with two problems that both arrive in January. A war that most Americans want ended. And files that a new Congress wants opened.

Because on the 3rd of January, the new Congress is sworn in, and Democrats get the power that matters most in Washington. Subpoenas. They can force people to testify and hand over documents. They have already said what they want to investigate. The decision to go to war with Iran without asking Congress. The handling of the Epstein files. The President's business interests. The top Democrat on the House Foreign Affairs Committee says that in January he will bring a war powers resolution and support cutting off money for the war. And with control of both chambers, Democrats can write into must-pass spending bills that no money may be used to fight Iran.

There are limits. The President still has a veto, and Democrats do not have enough votes to override it on their own. But they control the money, and money is what keeps three aircraft carriers at sea.

That is why the weeks before January matter so much. Some analysts say that if there is going to be a big new military move, it is likely to come before the new Congress takes over. And the President told Israeli television this week that he is not rushing, and that we will see what happens after the new year starts. Before that, there are two dates to watch. On the 29th of November, the OPEC plus oil producers decide how much oil to pump next year. And on the 11th of December, the government runs out of money, and Democrats have talked about using that deadline to pressure the President to end the war.

So what happens to oil in January? It comes down to three roads. If there is a deal, the blockade lifts and the strait reopens, even with a toll, and the calmer forecasts point toward the seventies, close to where oil was before the war began. If nothing changes, a frozen war where ships slip through in the dark, oil likely stays stuck around where it is now. And if the strikes start again before the new Congress arrives, analysts have warned that crude could jump toward one hundred and fifty. Even the big banks cannot agree. For the first three months of next year, BNP Paribas expects about seventy-eight dollars a barrel. Morgan Stanley says one hundred. That twenty-two dollar gap is the price of not knowing what happens in Washington.

But there is one price that will not come down on any of those roads. Diesel. Diesel moves the trucks that carry your groceries. The war hit refineries and fuel shipments even harder than crude, and the US government now expects diesel to average about five dollars a gallon next year. This Thanksgiving is already on track to be the most expensive ever at the pump.

So, are oil prices finally dropping? Yes. But not because the war is over. They are dropping because the world found a way around it, and because voters just took the steering wheel away from one side of Washington. Now a war that may have been launched in the shadow of the Epstein files could be ended, or escalated, in the shadow of the new Congress coming to open them.

The 29th of November. The 11th of December. The 3rd of January. Watch those three dates. When one of them breaks, the sign at your gas station will tell you before the news does.
