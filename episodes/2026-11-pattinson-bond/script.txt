You know the gun barrel, the shaken martini, the tuxedo and the Aston Martin. Only six men have ever worn that suit. And on Monday the seventh was named. He is the same man who once died in a maze at the end of a wizard tournament, sparkled in a forest as a vampire, stood in the rain in a black cowl, and went mad in a lighthouse beside a seagull. The studio did not pick him in spite of that strange road. It picked him because of it. This is the entire history of Robert Pattinson, the seventh James Bond.

Robert Douglas Thomas Pattinson was born on the 13th of May, 1986, in Barnes, a leafy corner of southwest London beside the Thames. His mother, Clare, worked for a modelling agency. His father, Richard, imported vintage cars from America. He grew up with two older sisters, Elizabeth and Victoria, and the family was comfortable but not famous, not even close. If you had asked anyone on his street what little Rob would become, nobody would have said movie star. He went to the Harrodian School, a private school in London, and he was the kind of kid who was more interested in music than in lessons. He taught himself piano and guitar, and he wrote songs in his bedroom long before he ever read a script.

When he was about twelve, his sister's modelling work opened a door, and he did a little modelling himself. It did not last, and the work dried up by the time he was fifteen. So at fifteen he did something small that changed everything. He joined a local amateur group called the Barnes Theatre Company, starting out backstage before taking small parts. He was not a star in that room. He was the kid who showed up. But when he played in their production of Tess of the d'Urbervilles, a talent agent saw him, and that was the first real door.

His first screen jobs were tiny. In 2004 he had a part in a German television fantasy called Ring of the Nibelungs, which in some countries was released as Sword of Xanten. Then came a much more painful early lesson. He filmed scenes for the film Vanity Fair, with Reese Witherspoon, and almost all of his scenes were cut from the final film. He had worked on a real movie and the audience barely saw him. For a long time he was an actor who auditioned a lot and booked very little.

Then, in 2005, came Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire. He was cast as Cedric Diggory, the handsome, decent Hufflepuff champion who is chosen alongside Harry for the Triwizard Tournament. Cedric was a perfect part for a young actor nobody had heard of. He is likeable, good at everything, and then at the end of the film he dies. Millions of people saw that scene. The internet called him the handsome Hufflepuff. For the first time, strangers knew his face, and none of them knew his name.

His next step was almost a dead end, and he has joked about that too. In the years after Harry Potter, he went to audition after audition and kept losing parts. In 2007 and 2008 he was playing small roles. He kept playing music at open mic nights in London, in small bars and pubs, and he kept going to castings.

In 2008 he walked into an audition for a film based on a young adult vampire novel, called Twilight. The studio was nervous about the project, and the budget was modest, about thirty-seven million dollars. The director was Catherine Hardwicke. He auditioned opposite Kristen Stewart, and the chemistry in that scene is the reason the whole thing worked. He got the part. The film came out in November 2008. It made roughly four hundred million dollars worldwide. Nobody at the studio had expected that.

Twilight changed his life in a few weeks. He went from a quiet actor nobody recognised to a man who could not walk down a street. Fans waited outside hotels. His face was on posters, on lunchboxes and on magazine covers around the world. Over the next four years, there were four more films, and the five Twilight films together made more than three billion dollars. He was one of the most famous young actors on earth, and for a while he was also one of the most uncomfortable. He gave interviews where he made fun of the character, and fans took it as either charming honesty or a betrayal, depending on who you asked. But the truth he has since explained is simple. He was worried that he had been stuck in one role, and he had no idea how to be anything else.

So he made a decision that most stars at that moment do not make. While Twilight was still running, he started choosing small films with hard directors. In 2012 he worked with David Cronenberg on Cosmopolis, a cold, strange film where he spent most of the movie in the back of a limousine. Critics were surprised. The teen idol could act, and he could be strange. He appeared in Water for Elephants, in Remember Me, in Bel Ami, and in each of them he was trying out a different version of himself. Not every film worked. Some were quickly forgotten. But he kept taking jobs where the pay was low and the director was someone he admired. That was the plan. He has described it as using the money and fame from Twilight to buy himself a different career.

That is why the next few years look so odd on paper. In 2014 he starred in The Rover, a dusty, violent Australian thriller from director David Michôd, where he played a slow, damaged young man named Rey. In the same year he was in Maps to the Stars with Cronenberg again. In 2016 he was part of the cast of The Lost City of Z, a jungle adventure film from director James Gray. These were films that his old fans did not go to see. Critics did, though, and a smaller audience began to notice that the vampire could do things the vampire could not.

The turning point came in 2017 with a film called Good Time, directed by Josh and Benny Safdie. He plays a small-time criminal named Connie who tries to rescue his brother after a bank robbery goes wrong, and the whole film is one long, frantic night in New York. He is sweaty, desperate and magnetic. The film played at the Cannes Film Festival and critics started to write that he was one of the best actors of his generation. People who had laughed at him for being a vampire were now repeating that sentence in newspapers. By his own account he reached out to the Safdie brothers himself, which tells you how he thought about his career at that point. He was not waiting to be offered things. He was going after them.

Then came a run of strange, ambitious films. In 2018 he starred in High Life, a cold, dark space film from French director Claire Denis. In 2019 he was in The King, a historical film with Timothée Chalamet, where he played the French Dauphin with a ridiculous accent and enormous confidence. And also in 2019 came The Lighthouse, the film that for many people is the moment he became an actor you could not ignore. It was directed by Robert Eggers and shot in black and white on film, in a cramped, wet, storm-hit setting in Nova Scotia, in Canada. He and Willem Dafoe play two lighthouse keepers who slowly go mad. There are long speeches, strange noises, sea gulls, and a lot of shouting at the weather. It was exhausting to make and strange to watch, and critics loved it. If you wanted a single film to prove he had escaped Twilight, this was it.

By now, big directors had noticed. In 2020 he was cast in Tenet, the Christopher Nolan thriller. He played Neil, a calm, elegant agent who helps the main character through a story that runs forwards and backwards at the same time. It was a supporting role, but a memorable one, and it put him next to one of the biggest directors in the world as a serious actor rather than a heartthrob. The film came out in the middle of the pandemic, so audiences were small, but the film still earned over three hundred million dollars at a time when most cinemas were closed.

And then came the part nobody expected. In May 2019, it was announced that he would be the next Batman. Warner Brothers had been looking for a new Bruce Wayne for Matt Reeves, who was directing a darker, more detective-style film. Many fans were not happy. Some online were still stuck on the sparkling vampire. A lot of people said that Twilight guy cannot be Batman. He did not argue with them. He simply got on with the job.

In 2020 the shoot started, and in September of that year production was suddenly stopped because he tested positive for Covid. The film was delayed. When The Batman finally came out in March 2022, it was a very different Batman from the ones people had seen before. It was long, rainy, quiet, almost a noir detective story. His Bruce Wayne was a young, damaged, sleepless recluse who barely leaves his house, and the film made about seven hundred and seventy million dollars worldwide. That is a very large number, and it meant he was now the leading man of one of the biggest film franchises in the world. The boy whose modelling work had dried up was now behind the most famous mask in comics.

He did not slow down after that. In 2025 came Mickey 17, directed by Bong Joon-ho, the Korean director who had won the Oscar for Parasite. He plays Mickey, a man who takes a dangerous job on a space mission where he can be killed and then printed back to life again, over and over. It was a strange, funny, tender film, and he played at least two versions of the same man at once, one meek and one loud. The box office did not match its budget, but many critics said it was one of his best performances. In the same period he starred opposite Jennifer Lawrence in Die, My Love, directed by Lynne Ramsay, a raw, uncomfortable film about a young couple and the strain of new parenthood.

Away from the camera, his life changed too. He and the actress and singer Suki Waterhouse have been together since 2018, and they have a daughter, who was born in March 2024. He has always kept his private life guarded, and he rarely talks about it in interviews, which makes it even more unusual that he has become one of the few film stars who is both very famous and hard to find. He has also kept up the music he started as a teenager. Two of his songs even appeared on the Twilight soundtrack, and he still plays guitar and piano.

This year, 2026, has been the busiest of his career. In April came The Drama, an A24 film with Zendaya about a couple whose wedding plans are shaken by a secret, with Pattinson and Zendaya as the engaged couple. In July came Christopher Nolan's The Odyssey, a huge adaptation of Homer's ancient epic, with Matt Damon, Tom Holland, Anne Hathaway and Zendaya, and Pattinson playing Antinous, the leader of the suitors trying to take over Odysseus's home. In December he is set to appear in Dune: Part Three, directed by Denis Villeneuve. And the Batman sequel has been pushed back again, to 2028.

Then, on the 16th of November, 2026, came the news that shook everything. Amazon MGM Studios announced that Robert Pattinson is the new James Bond. Denis Villeneuve, the director of Dune, will direct the film from a script by Steven Knight, and it will be the first Bond film made by Amazon. There is no title and no release date yet. He takes over from Daniel Craig, who played the part for five films. Villeneuve said he has the timeless soul of 007 and brings a spirit that is completely new, and Pattinson said that calling the role a dream come true is an understatement.

The idea is not new. Back in 2019, the director Danny Boyle, who had been set to direct a Bond film before he left the project, said after watching High Life that Pattinson should be Bond. At the time the actor had only just been named Batman, and many people were still picturing a vampire. Seven years later, the vampire has the licence to kill.

So look at the whole path from the start. A boy in Barnes whose modelling work dried up, a teenager in a small theatre group, a young man cut out of a film, a Hufflepuff who died in a maze. Then a vampire who made billions, who was never sure he wanted the job, and who then chose a decade of difficult films to prove he was more than that. A man who reached out to directors and asked for work, who was laughed at as Batman, and who now holds the most famous job in spy cinema. His whole career is the story of one stubborn decision, to turn fame into freedom. He did not run away from the vampire. He used the vampire to pay for everything that came afterward.

And that is the real history of Robert Pattinson. It is not about luck or looks. It is the story of an actor who kept choosing the hard role, until the hardest and most famous role of all chose him.
